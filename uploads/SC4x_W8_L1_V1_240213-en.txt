In this lesson, we're going to introduce the concept of supply
chain visibility.
Supply chain visibility gives organizations
mastery over their environment.
For example, it allows companies to identify and predict
changes in demand, supply shortages, or transportation
disruptions.
This information enables more responsive and agile operations
rather than just reacting to unexpected events.
It also allows companies to trace their products
and components from cradle to grave,
ensuring control over provenance,
preventing counterfeit, enabling compliance with regulations
and ESG requirements, and even facilitating reverse logistics
and circular economy models.
I know this all sounds great.
But achieving supply chain visibility is not an easy feat.
Recent surveys show that most companies do not
have a fully mapped supply chain network.
And visibility beyond Tier 1, Tier 2 suppliers
is rare to find.
Lack of collaboration, data sharing, and technology
integration are some common challenges
to end-to-end visibility.
In this lesson, we will clarify the terminology
around supply chain visibility and propose
a simple definition.
Then we will introduce a framework
to implement and evaluate supply chain visibility initiatives.
And we'll discuss relevant technologies
for supply chain visibility.
Finally, we will wrap up with some strategic considerations
and key takeaways from the lesson.
