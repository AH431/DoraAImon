INMA BORRELLA: In the previous segments,
we discussed how to access information
and how to make sure that the information is good enough
to generate insights.
Now we will dive into the last element of the supply chain
visibility framework, utilization of information.
The effects of improved supply chain visibility
are expected to be new or improved
supply chain capabilities and operational efficiencies.
Capability is considered to be a direct effect of supply chain
visibility, while performance is the consequence of improved
supply chain capabilities.
For example, better visibility may lead to a more agile supply
chain that can readjust transportation routing
in real time to avoid disruptions,
such as traffic jams or road closures, that
will cause delays.
This agility will lead to better delivery on time ratios
and higher customer satisfaction-- therefore,
supply chain performance.
Supply chain visibility reduces silos
and generates insights that can create better planning
capabilities, enhanced strategic decision-making, stronger
supply chain relationships and trust, better resilience
through the understanding of systemic risks, agility,
and flexibility.
These capabilities will translate
into operational performance improvements.
So let's go through some examples
of this operational performance improvement
that visibility may bring to the supply chain.
First, insights into lead times will
reduce variability and inventory buffers,
leading to less inventory holding costs.
Better control over inventory will
lead to better service levels.
Accurate data on the content of shipments
will lead to fewer penalties.
Visibility at the purchase order level
will allow for load consolidation, which may
translate into cost savings.
Automation of tracking processes will
lead to fewer errors and reduced overhead costs.
And one final example, visibility
over potential disruptions, will lead to timely action
to mitigate risk.
